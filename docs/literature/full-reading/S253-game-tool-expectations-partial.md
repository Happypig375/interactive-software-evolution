## Method and units

Section3 describes purposive selection of seven professional organizations in southeastern Finland from research partners and additional volunteers. Cases deliberately vary in platform, business maturity and production scale. This is a heterogeneous qualitative sample, not a representative industry survey or non-game comparison. Production-team size counts everyone contributing to a product; the five-person minimum and several-hundred-person maximum therefore must not be silently read as employee counts of the sampled legal organizations.

Four role-oriented rounds target project management, development/testing, upper management/ownership and game/art design. One organization has three rounds because of scheduling, with the missing second-round topics addressed elsewhere. Thus **27 interview sessions**, sometimes with one or two organizational participants, do **not** establish27 unique developers. The sessions occur during spring–fall2012 and total about1,400 recorded minutes. All interviewees are described as adult full-time professionals. The paper reports seven questionnaire designers, six interviewers in its validity discussion and three analysts working separately; it supplies no independent duplicate-coding or agreement statistic.

The analysis follows seeded open, axial and selective grounded-theory coding. It reports172 coding entries across1,574 transcript excerpts, relates tool/process categories and creates an umbrella tool-suitability category because none of the narrower categories explains the whole phenomenon. These are coded qualitative units, not1,574 independent cases or experimentally identified causes. The researchers explicitly say the data do not explain why developers are generally satisfied. The claim of satisfaction and a causal explanation for it remain separate.

Textual discrepancies remain uncorrected: the prose describes two experienced organizations with more than ten releases, while extracted Table2 lists A above ten and C below ten. Section4.1 says six categories but describes five concrete Table3 fields alongside the overarching category. Full visual/edition verification and the underlying packet are needed before resolving these differences. They do not erase the consistent positive accounts.

## What the cases actually contribute

| Reported case or pattern | Supported interpretation and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Engine reuse, §4.2.2/Table3 | Five cases currently use third-party engines (A/B/C/D/F), three with local modifications (A/C/F). E and G use their own; B has recently switched and E intends to switch. These distinguish past, current and intended adoption. B reports a substantial improvement despite occasional crashes; G remains an own-engine exception. No timed migration, equal-resource comparator or controlled quality gain is measured. |
| Choice constraints, §4.2.3 | Platform support is necessary; compatibility, familiarity/preferences, portability and staffing availability also matter. Only B explicitly raises price when explaining its selection. This does not show that other firms have no budget constraint or that license price exhausts adoption cost. |
| Prototyping and design, §4.2.4 | A/C use working prototypes with recycled assets; D uses development tools during design. B/D also use screen mockups, sometimes followed by prototypes. D's design document changes even in core mechanics. These are concrete reported practices, not measured iteration-speed effects. |
| Usability and defects, §4.2.1 | A wants feedback attached to the relevant location inside a level instead of ambiguous spreadsheet descriptions. C wants more intuitive tools; D reports editor restrictions/unstable versions; B wants fewer bugs and better version control. Favorable overall infrastructure judgments coexist with specific improvement needs. |
| Team workflow, §5 | Version control/file sharing support all organizations. Small teams report useful informal communication and sometimes abandon management tools as extra work; larger teams use more unified tooling/processes. The authors expect growth to change coordination needs. This is conditional process fit, not a universal argument against documentation or project management. |
| Assets and tool categories, §5/Table4 | Many buy assets while retaining design/core development; only one aims to do everything internally. The text distinguishes design/prototyping, implementation and support tools across preproduction/production, including graphics/audio, documents, engine, version control and sharing. It does not give a numeric ranking, savings estimate or substitute for the unviewed layout. |

These accounts preserve favorable third-party reuse and established tooling, alongside unmet needs. They are2012 observations of selected organizations, with interviews rather than direct task-performance observation. The study does not evaluate Nu, F#, functional state, live migration, a tool intervention or lifecycle maintenance effort. Its comments on differing older studies cannot identify a historical improvement trend because their samples and instruments differ.
