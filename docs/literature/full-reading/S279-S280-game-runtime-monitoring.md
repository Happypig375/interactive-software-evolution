## Publication lineage and case accounting

S280 evaluates five open-source games: Pacman Canvas, Infinite Mario Bros., Pingus, Chocolate Doom and Bos Wars (pp.145–146). S279 adds Unity's Angry Bots, giving six games in its abstract, Table I and §5. Its conclusion nevertheless says the setup was tested on **five** games (p.24), an internal denominator inconsistency. [S281](S281-game-monitoring-thesis.md) studies Infinite Mario, Pyramid Rising 2, Pingus and Bos Wars. The three documents therefore cover seven distinct named games in total, with substantial shared cases and method lineage; their publication count is not a replication count.

The overlap is concrete. S280's Pingus P1/P5 timing curves reappear as S279 Figures 9–10 with the same axes, shapes and approximate values, and the thesis reports the same Pingus rates. S279 was received in May 2014, revised in August and accepted in September, and is best treated as an expanded journal account of the conference-era work unless trace identities establish otherwise. It adds a sixth game, more formulas, Bos Wars measurements, automated filing, Replayr and failure-scope discussion. S281 contributes an earlier four-case thesis account and explicit finite-prefix semantics, including the commercial Pyramid case. These additions widen evidence and expose version differences; they do not create independent repeats of the shared Pingus or Bos Wars observations.

## Observation, ordering and temporal verdicts

The architecture has two specifications: an XML template selecting game state and an LTL-FO+ formula over the resulting snapshot sequence (S280 pp.147–149; S279 pp.10–17). The game opens a Unix named pipe, renders one snapshot during each instrumented update, and writes it to a separate BeepBeep process. In Pingus, the game loop calls `PinguHolder::update`, while the character modifications and export hook are placed in `pingu_holder.cpp`; the printed listing elides the dictionary-fill body, so it does not fully locate observation relative to every internal mutation. The event includes IDs, action, alive flag, position, velocity, ground type, a message number and timing fields. The monitor sees the exported sequence, not transient states that arise and disappear between two snapshots. It also cannot recover an omitted field or infer that a missing entity persisted.

The papers describe the pipe as one-way and asynchronous, so a verdict does not change game state. They also state that a full 64KB pipe makes writes block and can freeze the game (S279 p.20). Process separation therefore removes synchronous monitor calls under the observed empty-buffer condition; it does not make serialization, queueing, OS scheduling or backpressure free. The papers do not report dropped/reordered-message checks, partial-write handling or a latency distribution.

S279 closes the conference's formula omission for several Pingus properties (pp.15–16):

- P2 and P3 bind a character in one snapshot and universally quantify the matching ID in the next. If that ID is absent, the printed universal consequent can be true over an empty set; disappearance is not separately rejected.
- P4 requires each observed Basher, from the next snapshot, eventually to have a universally non-`basher` action. Disappearance can again satisfy the inner universal. More importantly, the prose says bashing through the end of a level indicates a problem, but the paper gives no level-end rule that turns an unfinished `F` obligation into false.
- P5 binds `deadlyvelocity` outside the temporal operator, so the threshold is taken from the first event. A too-fast Faller's next exported action must be `faller`, `drown` or `splashed`; absence has the same empty-domain issue.
- P6 compares a Walker and Blocker at equal coordinates and requires the next horizontal-velocity ratio to be negative. The formula captures a sign change, but zero-denominator behavior and disappearance are not discussed.

S279 Figure 5 prints recursive trace satisfaction rules but does not define finite-prefix finalization. Figure 6 visibly shows yellow question-mark statuses for two properties after 3,857 events and a red violation for another, which is operational evidence of an unresolved verdict state during execution. S281, pp.33–36, explicitly explains three-valued finite-prefix semantics. That thesis clarification supports the conceptual distinction, but neither publication states how the reported experiments scored obligations still inconclusive when a level or acquisition ended.

The GUI offers add, delete and “Reset all monitors” controls, and the studies say each played level's complete trace was saved. They do not state a reset schedule, whether reset discards pending obligations, whether the first post-reset snapshot reinitializes thresholds, or whether level end is itself an event. P4's intended “until end of level” behavior therefore remains under-specified. A Nu comparison needs an explicit observation phase, entity-disappearance policy, end/reset event, and disposition of pending `X`, `F` and `U` obligations.

## Fault evidence and denominators

The positive result is bounded but real: testers manually played the games, the monitor reportedly caught the selected faults when they occurred, and the Bos Wars cases came from historical tracker reports rather than being newly seeded. S280 says the first four games used purposefully inserted faults and Bos Wars used three gameplay entries selected from a 36-entry tracker (§V, pp.150–151). S279 says the remaining five of six games used inserted faults and reports detection in every game (§§5, 5.4, pp.17–22). In both, only Pingus's four inserted behaviors are enumerated. Counts and selection procedures for seeded faults in the other games, the number of runs/levels, false positives, opportunities where a fault did not occur, and a held-out defect-recall denominator are absent. The selected bugs were intentionally easy to see and usually introduced with one or two changed lines.

The Bos Wars labels also change across editions. S280 lists 29095 as engineer auto-repair versus recycling, 29765 as jet fighters shooting each other and 37861 as a target command making engineers harvest buildings (p.150). S279 instead states 29095 as salvaging a building under construction, 29765 as simultaneous salvage/repair and 37861 as harvesting buildings (p.8). S281 and the inspected formulas preserve further 29095/29765/37861 tension. Without accessible tracker pages or a version-to-issue mapping, successful detection should be attributed to the printed conditions and selected historical lineage, not a harmonized issue identity.

The journal adds two scoped demonstrations. BeepBeep successfully filed detected Pingus and Chocolate Doom violations into Mantis using the property caption and formula (§5.5, pp.22–23), advancing beyond S280's future-work statement. Replayr recorded each Chocolate Doom screenshot alongside its XML event and allowed frame stepping (§5.7, pp.23–24); the authors observed no slowdown but report no timing or storage measurements. Neither demonstration establishes deduplication, report usefulness, saved repair time or long-term adoption.

## Cost boundaries

S280 measures Pingus on Ubuntu 12.04.2, Intel dual-core P7350 2GHz and 4GB RAM. It reports 167 snapshots/s, approximately 700 bytes/snapshot, 0.4ms game-side instrumentation CPU time/snapshot, 0.13ms monitor time for P1 and an early 0.3ms/snapshot region for P5. The inferred 179 uninstrumented updates/s and 6.7% overhead, the 39.4fps illustration and capacity above 50 P5-like properties at 60fps are calculations, not matched executions.

S279 adds important variation. Table II reports average message sizes of 100–51,567 bytes across the six games, including 285 bytes for Pingus and 51,567 for Bos Wars, while §5.1 again uses roughly 700-byte Pingus and 50KB Bos Wars events. Bos Wars emits about 54 events/s and takes about 5ms/snapshot for game-side XML work; the article infers 68 uninstrumented events/s and 17.6% overhead, then calculates a 30-to-25.5fps (14%) example. The 64KB pipe's “90 events” margin is a Pingus-sized calculation and does not describe a 50KB Bos Wars event stream. Input buffer occupancy was observed as zero in the reported runs, a favorable result without a distribution or stress boundary.

The journal and thesis report different Bos Wars percentage summaries (the thesis table says approximately 7%). No trace identity or procedural reconciliation justifies averaging them. Message construction, property processing, Mantis calls, screenshot capture, saved-trace storage and human work also sit in different cost scopes. Beyond the under-one-hour final Bos Wars instrumentation/property task, the papers do not measure specification design, schema maintenance, formula debugging, total integration work, comparative tester time or net maintenance cost. One representative trace is plotted after manual inspection found collected traces “very similar”; the number and dispersion of those traces are not reported.

Parent review also checked the journal's printed Bos Wars arithmetic on p.20.
The displayed 68→54 Hz endpoints imply a 20.6% frequency reduction, rather than
17.6%; 30→25.5 fps implies 15%, rather than 14%. Under the paper's simple
additive-delay model, adding 5ms to a 30Hz loop instead gives about26.1Hz. These
are calculations from rounded reported quantities, not corrected experimental
observations. Preserve the paper's numbers as reported and do not import their
percentage examples as reconciled cost estimates.

For Nu, the useful comparison is therefore exported-state fidelity, exact ordering and phase, monitor/history lifetime, finite-trace policy, backpressure and total integration/maintenance work. The feasibility timings and selected detections are positive evidence. They do not isolate a language effect or establish whole-program preservation after an edit.

## Disposition and consequential continuation

The exact S279/S280 body and visual-access gaps are resolved. Preserve the positive multi-game feasibility, selected-fault detection, automated filing and replay evidence together with their scopes. S279 is a meaningful expansion, while its shared cases, curves and dates prevent counting it as an independent replication of S280; S281 has the same constraint for its overlapping cases.

The changed design consequence is narrower and more concrete than the earlier reconstruction: P4's formula is now known, but end-of-level/reset policy is still missing; event sizes and overhead vary substantially across games; pipe backpressure is a stated boundary; and journal additions have distinct cost scopes. A future Nu observation study should predeclare the exported fields and phase, per-entity disappearance semantics, FIFO/loss checks, reset/finalization rule, and which game-side, monitor-side, storage and human work counts. Detection should be scored against assigned opportunities and all attempted traces, not publication or case counts.

Unresolved limits are the experimental source/binary-to-trace identity, exact number and dispersion of runs, full seeded-fault denominator outside Pingus, false-positive and missed-opportunity counts, Bos Wars issue mapping, finite-trace scoring, matched performance baselines and total maintenance benefit. The 2013 predecessor [DOI10.1007/978-3-642-40787-1_23](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-40787-1_23) is only consequential if one of those specific method questions requires its edition. No further acquisition, execution or experiment follows from this reading.
