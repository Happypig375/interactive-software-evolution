## S272: cases, architecture reconstruction and quality model

S272 retrospectively studies five Windows game projects made by one small team, including two paper authors, at Spell of Play Studios: Time Breaker (2005, published), Frontline (2006 prototype), TWTPB (2008, published and maintained to 2010), Hero (2009 prototype) and Gears of Love (2010 prototype). The cases were chosen to vary age, genre and completeness. There is no independent allocation or external comparison population. The authors inspect C++ source and generated dependency graphs, build UML diagrams and responsibility logs, tag classes, synthesize architectural variants, and compare their separation of model, view, controller and rendering services. Game-rule design is expressly outside the analysis. **Source locator:** S272 PDF pp. 1-2, Sections I-II.A.

Three variants are reconstructed. Time Breaker and Frontline separate the model from a monolithic user interface but mix view and controller duties. TWTPB uses a supervising controller and a partial, coarse view layer, with view responsibilities still inside controller classes. Hero and Gears of Love use supervising controllers behind a stricter view layer; Hero uses finer-grained views. In every variant, an `EventTarget`-like interface mediates model-to-interface events and interface-to-model input. The later projects still have indirect dependencies, identifiers and duplicated representations; the paper does not claim perfect conformance. **Source locator:** S272 Figures 1-3 and Sections III.A-III.C, PDF pp. 3-4.

The quality model operationalizes five locally authored goals: allocation by expertise, movement between projects, portability, insulation from rendering changes, and ease of trying features. Its measures are efferent-coupling inequality via the Gini coefficient, duplicated implementation lines, and lines of code directly or indirectly using rendering. CppDepend 2.8.5.0 excludes engine coupling and zero-coupling classes from the Gini calculation; Duplo measures `.cpp` duplication, supplemented by manual inspection of duplicated state/content. The final score is the sum of ordinal ranks across ECGC, relative duplicated LoC and the relative indirectly-but-not-directly-rendering-dependent interface code. This is a study-specific structural proxy, not a direct observation of portability, feature effort or developer productivity. **Source locator:** S272 Section II.B, PDF pp. 2-3; Tables II-IV, PDF p. 5.

The ordinal ranking favors Hero (sum 4), Gears of Love (5), refactored TWTPB (11), Frontline (13), Time Breaker (14) and original TWTPB (15). The study therefore supplies a scoped positive association between the later strict-view variants and its own quality model. Project age, genre, engine, evolving team knowledge and author participation remain entangled. The authors explicitly acknowledge that their model may encode their prior belief that the later architecture is better. **Source locator:** S272 Tables II-V and Section IV, PDF pp. 5-6; threats, PDF p. 7.

## S272: refactoring, reported outcomes and limits

The authors apply their earlier conformance method to TWTPB, identify 23 interface types, perform three type splits, six type moves, four function moves and ten encapsulations, rerun conformance checking and test the game. They report **nine days** of refactoring; splitting the gameplay, asset-editor and menu types was most troublesome. Four reuse candidates were further isolated but not moved into the engine. This is a concrete change cost, performed by authors with prior project knowledge rather than by independent maintainers. **Source locator:** S272 Section II.C, PDF p. 3; Table I and Section III.D, PDF p. 4.

The refactoring leaves duplicated LoC unchanged at 155; ECGC improves from 0.781 to 0.738; directly-rendering-dependent LoC falls from 2,411 to 2,048; indirectly dependent LoC changes from 2,698 to 2,712. Controller LoC rises from 287 to 664 as responsibilities are separated. For 13 compared classes, mean coupling falls from 21.3 to 18.5; the paper's one-tailed dependent test reports `t=-1.48`, `p=0.08`, and the highest-coupled class falls from 64 to 46. Total coupling actually rises slightly, 277 to 280, because the split adds a view class with coupling 37. These results support redistribution under the selected metrics, not a large or conventionally significant overall reduction. **Source locator:** S272 Table II and outcome prose, PDF p. 5.

The paper estimates that strict view/controller separation might save up to 15% of effort when porting rendering services. That number is inferred from structural code proportions, then immediately qualified for indirect dependencies and rule/input changes; it is not a measured porting task. The team's Xbox 360 experience is an experience report that game rules also had to change. No port duration, maintenance-task correctness, runtime behavior oracle, developer sample or matched cost comparator is supplied. **Source locator:** S272 discussion, PDF p. 6.

## S273: pattern-level conformance mechanism

S273 defines a source-code element as belonging to one architectural element and distinguishes source dependencies from intended architectural dependencies. It compares inspections, Reflexion Models, dependency rules and dependency-structure matrices. Reflexion Models offer visual abstraction but can hide element-level mandatory-dependency failures; dependency rules are expressive and service-friendly but normally require roughly `N x (N-1)` pairwise architectural rules. The thesis selects a rule-based foundation and raises it to a pattern-specific heuristic. **Source locator:** S273 printed pp. 17-25 / PDF pp. 23-31, especially Table 3.1.

For each code element, `DevC` infers the developer's intended MVC element from names or containment; `ArchC` infers the architectural role from dependency-graph distance to a parameterized external user-interface API (`UIAPI`): direct use is View, indirect use is Controller, and no path is Model. If classifications differ, a table maps the pair to an absent or divergent dependency. The UIAPI provides the ground truth and must be adjustable as the platform evolves. A breadth-first path calculation implements the distance. The hypothetical examples cover a conformant dice game, a model-to-controller cycle, a misplaced observer interface, view responsibility in controller/model, a reusable view-side class and a new architectural layer. **Source locator:** S273 Algorithm 1, Table 3.2 and Figures 3.3-3.9, printed pp. 26-35 / PDF pp. 32-41.

The method is deliberately incomplete. It excludes logical or hidden dependencies encoded as strings/IDs from its base graph; painted primitive types, database/file/schema relations, UI-specific model structures and mixed performance representations can evade it. One real violation can also propagate secondary reports along paths. Interfaces, passing-only data classes, tests and exceptions can look nonconformant without representing erosion. An architecture without a dependency-based unambiguous role cannot supply `ArchC`. **Source locators:** dependency scope, printed p. 19 / PDF p. 25; evaluation failures, Tables 4.5-4.6, printed p. 43 / PDF p. 49; problematic situations, printed pp. 69-70 / PDF pp. 75-76.

## S273: inspection comparison and refactoring evaluation

The evaluation defines erosion through a manual protocol: illegal dependency, model logic in the UI, a UI-specific model, an incohesive model, or a painted type. Two people inspect every type and manually supply `DevC`; one unmodified CPPDepend 3.1 query computes distance to the `dxrnd` UIAPI for `ArchC`. The four C++ games are Gears of Love, Pirate Quest, Hero and TWTPB, all using the same EngineIII/DirectX context. Copy-protection and test elements are excluded. Manual inspection took about **180 person-hours over six weeks**. It is the study oracle, but it is subjective and may itself miss erosion. **Source locator:** S273 Table 4.1, Listing 4.1 and Table 4.2, printed pp. 37-41 / PDF pp. 43-47.

Across the four projects the method reports 76 true positives, 18 false positives and 25 false negatives against that oracle: precision **0.81** and recall **0.75**. Project precision ranges 0.76-0.90 and recall 0.63-0.88. The thesis occasionally calls this “five cases,” but Table 4.3 contains four projects; retain the table's actual denominator. Eighteen of the 25 false negatives are said to be addressable by broader/flexible UIAPI definitions, but that is a proposed refinement, not a rerun outcome. Harder misses include a whole subgame implemented in UI code, audio IDs from model to UI, and a world representation mixing play/edit needs. **Source locator:** S273 Table 4.3, printed p. 41 / PDF p. 47; Tables 4.5-4.6, printed p. 43 / PDF p. 49; summary, printed p. 46 / PDF p. 52.

Gears of Love is refactored in one working day; TWTPB takes nine. In TWTPB the three split-type changes dominate effort, while six DirectInput-related reuse candidates are only moved into the local UIAPI namespace because generalizing them would take more time. The later thesis report gives changed-type coupling sum 298 to 254, mean 19.87 to 18.14 and median 14 to 9.5; executable LoC changes from 5,154 to 5,168, with View 2,411 to 2,048, Controller 287 to 664 and Model unchanged at 2,456. Figure 4.2 calls the distribution change small and the author says a more specialized-view refactoring would have been better. **Source locator:** S273 Tables 4.4 and 4.7-4.8 and Figure 4.2, printed pp. 42-45 / PDF pp. 48-51.

These TWTPB numbers share the same underlying refactoring as S272 but are not numerically identical: S272 reports mean coupling 21.3 to 18.5 and total 277 to 280 for 13 classes, whereas S273 reports 19.87 to 18.14 and total 298 to 254 over 15/14 changed elements; S272 total executable LoC is 5,654/5,656 while S273 gives 5,154/5,168. The sources do not reconcile the changed-element sets or totals. Treat them as two reports in one study lineage, not independent replications or interchangeable tables.

## S273: Rulzor field experiment

Rulzor implements the heuristic for PHP as a GitHub-integrated service. A webhook triggers analysis on pushes to the main branch; PHP-Parser 0.9.4 builds a dependency graph; classification compares the name/path/namespace `DevC` heuristic with the dependency `ArchC` heuristic; and issue management opens or closes GitHub issues. Developers can mark an issue “won't fix” to suppress re-reporting. The PHP UIAPI comprises HTTP globals and functions plus a crude HTML-string test. Tests and exceptions are excluded after pre-experiment false positives, and path logic suppresses selected cascading reports. One dynamic-class-name construct caused an unhandled analyzer exception and a short outage for one project during the experiment. **Source locator:** S273 Figures 5.1-5.3, Table 5.2 and Listings 5.2-5.3, printed pp. 50-55 / PDF pp. 56-61.

The experiment occurs in a second-year PHP course. Individual projects last 3-4 weeks and determine about 65% of the course grade. MVC is mandatory; students receive prior lectures/assignments, may not use PHP frameworks, have a mid-project staff check and ordinary tutoring, and can remove the webhook. Course staff do not know allocations and are instructed not to help with experiment-specific matters. Eleven volunteers are randomized six to tool and five to control. One student from each arm lacks the final survey and one 358-LoC tool project is removed, leaving **four projects per arm**. The thesis gives no intention-to-treat result for all randomized projects. **Source locator:** S273 printed pp. 53-55 / PDF pp. 59-61 and Table 5.3.

For each retained project, the authors sample types, LoC and reported violations at the end of each day, carry the previous value across inactive days, trim initial inactivity and normalize project duration. This yields 97 tool-project days and 94 control-project days. A Wilcoxon-Mann-Whitney test over those daily observations reports `p=0.000225`, `U=3176.5`, `Z=-3.6588`, `r=0.26`; median violations are 3 versus 5. Final grades average 4.25 versus 4.0; LoC and type-count comparisons are nonsignificant (`p=0.15` and `p=0.082`). **Source locator:** S273 printed pp. 54-57 / PDF pp. 60-63, Figures 5.4-5.6 and Equation 5.1.

The positive finding is narrowly that tool-arm histories contain fewer **reported heuristic violations**. The allocation unit is the project/student, but the significance test treats repeated daily observations within eight projects, including some carried-forward inactive-day values, as observations; the thesis does not model within-project dependence or report how many rows were carried forward. The outcome is not manually adjudicated erosion, runtime behavior, task success, time, or later maintenance. One tool participant says they did not actively correct reports; no retained project eliminates every violation; issue suppression is unused; and violations rise toward deadlines. Figure 5.7's stronger curve after removing that participant is descriptive post hoc evidence, not a new randomized comparison. The author explicitly limits transfer to small, individual student projects and warns that frameworks, experienced developers, larger systems, performance and special cases may change the result. **Source locator:** S273 printed pp. 57-59 / PDF pp. 63-65; validity, printed pp. 73-77 / PDF pp. 79-83.

## Publication and case lineage

S273 is a synthesis and extension, not an independent replication of S272. The thesis states that Chapters 2 and 4 are mostly based on the published 2014 QoSA paper and S272, with discussion and validity material carried forward; Chapter 5 corresponds to an accepted ECSA workshop paper. Thesis Chapter 2 repeats S272's five-game, same-team architectural history. Thesis Chapter 4 reuses TWTPB, Hero and Gears of Love, adds Pirate Quest, and applies the earlier conformance method to a closely related in-house game line. Only Chapter 5 supplies a new student/web-project population. Count the works separately as editions/publications, but do not multiply the shared game cases or refactoring as independent evidence. **Source locator:** S273 publication statement, printed pp. 6-7 / PDF pp. 12-13; bibliography entries `OTWE14` and `OTWE15`, printed p. 89 / PDF p. 95.

## Consequences for the Nu research designs

These readings strengthen a structural comparator for architecture-related maintenance. A bounded Nu task could predefine intended component responsibilities, external-engine/API dependencies and a naming/containment mapping, then score both structural violations and manual adjudication. It should measure precision/recall, setup and tuning work, investigation of false reports, refactoring work and final behavior separately. S273's `DevC`/`ArchC` mismatch is a useful mechanism-level comparator because it exposes disagreement between declared organization and dependency behavior without pretending that either alone is the oracle.

The evidence does **not** show that MVC conformance preserves gameplay, live state or temporal behavior. It also does not validate a general maintenance benefit. S272's portability estimate is unmeasured; S273's refactoring metrics come from one author-involved game; and the student experiment observes rule-report counts rather than maintenance outcomes. A Nu comparison should therefore retain ordinary manual inspection/refactoring as a valid alternative and include executable behavior observations if the task claims behavior. It should not use final tests to teach or stop a candidate, or treat fewer static warnings as success.

The most useful scoped findings are positive and adverse together: pattern-specific rules can be automated and find many manually judged structural problems; tool-arm student histories contain fewer reported violations; manual inspection and late separation take substantial work; and the heuristic misses logical/data dependencies, emits false reports and needs project-specific UIAPI maintenance. The author's expectation of less value for experienced developers is not a measured experience interaction. These findings support measuring total work and diagnostic accuracy. They do not establish Nu novelty, a dedicated Nu checker, or superiority of any architectural style.
