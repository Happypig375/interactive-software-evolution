# S257 — Unreal Blueprints and C++ in professional task work

Jose Ignacio Trasobares, África Domingo, Jorge Echeverría, Lorena Arcega and Carlos Cetina, *Deepening Our Understanding on the Use of Models and Code in Game Software Engineering: A Controlled Experiment in Unreal Engine*, MODELS 2025, pp. 107–118, [DOI 10.1109/MODELS67397.2025.00016](https://doi.org/10.1109/MODELS67397.2025.00016). Main-agent reconstruction, 2026-10-04. **Complete selected author edition:** all 12 pages, six figures, three tables, model equations and 48 references. All page visuals inspected; pages 3, 5, 7 and 8 enlarged. The earlier package paper receives an edition comparison, not a second full-publication credit. Publisher-final correspondence remains unverified.

This study supplies a contemporary tool-integrated comparison: 31 Unreal professionals complete prepared implementation tasks with Blueprints and C++. Blueprints improve the study's correctness score; the selected model finds no significant efficiency or satisfaction contrast. Practitioner accounts favor combining the two tools and describe both rapid experimentation and maintenance difficulties. Those accounts help identify plausible mechanisms and integration obligations, without establishing which mechanism caused the score difference or a net lifecycle benefit for Nu.

## What participants receive and do

The sample contains **20 developers and 11 artists** from 15 companies, recruited through studios, public mailing lists and social networks. One separate pilot participant is excluded. Three online Teams sessions have 15, 5 and 11 analyzed participants. Two instructors provide instructions/support and conduct discussion; three take notes. The focus report records €70 per participant for two hours. The paper's seven-junior/23-senior prose sums to 30; its released dataset and focus-report Table 1 instead establish **seven juniors and 24 seniors**. Recruitment refusals, the total invited population and any earlier attrition are not enumerated.

Both treatments use Unreal 5.3.2; C++ uses Visual Studio Community 2022, reported version 17.6.5. Participants receive a prepared project with shared base assets and separate Blueprint/code exercise folders. Forms identify exact files, required operations and videos of the intended behavior. The English deck explicitly prohibits external aids, including AI and forums. Questions and setup help are permitted. This is supported completion of prepared functionality, not unrestricted production development or a comparison of bare language syntax.

1. **T1, inventory search:** free choice of Blueprint and/or C++, used for preference and environment familiarization. Find an item by ID, expose its information and report success/failure. Configuration issues can be resolved here. T1 is outside the two-task outcome comparison, so the reported efficiency does not amortize this setup period.
2. **T2, movement:** implement controller-relative movement from Enhanced Input. Form A directs Blueprints in `BP_BPCustomPlayer`; form B directs C++ `OnMove` in `customPlayer.cpp`, including conversion to a two-dimensional input vector. Both require correct directions and controller rotation; the Blueprint form also requires connected/organized nodes and the C++ form clean compilable code.
3. **T3, damage:** health decreases while the player touches fire and stops decreasing after exit. Form A directs C++ overlap callbacks, timer start/stop and damage handling in `DamageZone.cpp`; form B directs Blueprint overlap events, a timer handle and timer clearing in `BP_DamageZone`.

Random assignment counterbalances **method sequence**, with A doing Blueprint→C++ and B doing C++→Blueprint. Everyone does movement before damage. Task and period are therefore linked. Each task records self-reported start/end clock times, sends modified files as a ZIP, and asks about method use, difficulties and satisfaction. The forms ask whether the other method was used despite the assigned-method instruction; compliance answers are not reconstructed from the inspected aggregate dataset. Gameplay videos are disclosed task information. We do not assume hidden tests, a fixed repair budget, independent behavioral testing or completion criteria beyond the documented procedure.

## What correctness and efficiency mean

`Rubric.xlsx`, `All Data` row 4, assigns T2 **seven equally averaged fields** per method: controller rotation, two direction vectors, two movement calls/blocks, axes, and a separate **Works** item. T3 similarly averages seven fields, covering character guards, timer start/stop, damage/variables and **Works**, with representation-specific operations. The `Resumen` participant totals are all zero in this released template; it is not a released participant-level adjudication trace. Raw item scores and a definition of all behavior checked by Works are not recovered.

Thus correctness includes expected implementation structure as well as a functional item. The temporal requirement—damage stops after exit—is real, but a timer field or successful compilation alone does not establish that every entry/exit ordering, duplicate overlap or pending callback was checked. This is a useful oracle precedent and a limit on interpretation, not a finding that the authors omitted all behavioral checking. Behaviorally equivalent alternative implementations and the rule for scoring them are not documented in the inspected material.

The outcome workbook has **62 populated rows, two per person, with 31 observations per method**. Converting stored correctness fractions to percentages and efficiency fractions to percentage-points/minute reproduces the headline means below. Four C++ rows have time, correctness and efficiency all zero; nine Blueprint and 14 C++ rows have zero correctness. Zero-time handling is not explained by these records. All rows remain in our descriptive check: no exclusion, replacement, imputation or favorable-case filtering. Nonzero-time efficiency equals correctness/time. The supplied `SqrtEFFICIENCY` column is the square root of percentage efficiency; the selected reported model instead uses its fourth root.

These preserve the positive correctness result and the inconclusive efficiency/satisfaction contrasts. Null significance is not equivalence or noninferiority. The all-row mean recorded time is 15.065 versus 14.645 minutes, including the four zero-time C++ records; it is not a net production-cost estimate. Developer means favor Blueprints in correctness (65.786 vs 37.157), as do artist means (20.130 vs 2.597). Nonsignificant method×role interactions do not establish identical benefits across roles or improvement for every individual. Role and prior experience are observational characteristics, not randomized treatments.

The reported mixed model includes method, role, experience, sequence, period and session, with method×role/experience/session interactions and subject repeated measures using unstructured covariance. Authors compare candidate models using residual checks and AIC/BIC. Workbook `4PAPER` matches Table II's p-values. Sheet `ALL` gives the selected correctness model the smallest AIC/BIC; the results prose's **highest** conflicts with the methods' **lowest**. Selection is not uniformly explained by the stated rule: fourth-root efficiency model 3 has lower AIC/BIC (115.526/123.628) than selected model 6 (120.341/127.989), with both Kolmogorov–Smirnov entries above .05. Shapiro–Wilk entries do not uniformly pass. Preserve this model-selection/reporting uncertainty rather than invent a selection rule; no model is rerun or independently calibrated.

Two Table I subgroup usefulness cells repeat ease-of-use values: artist C++ PU prints 2.73±1.42 but outcome rows give **2.955±1.135**; senior C++ PU prints 2.77±1.36 but rows give **2.948±1.189**. The overall method means remain consistent. Junior intention-to-use is slightly higher for C++ in both table and data, so broad prose claiming every subgroup/outcome favors Blueprints should not be adopted literally. These bounded corrections do not erase the primary result.

## Practice evidence, editions and consequence for Nu

The focus groups last approximately 30 minutes each. They are not recorded; instructors compile anonymous notes, and one researcher extracts recurring ideas. Contributions cannot be linked to individual task outcomes. The released report is a synthesis with selected quotations, not a participant-level transcript or prevalence estimate.

Participants describe exposing C++ variables to Blueprints for tuning, using Blueprints for gameplay/UI interaction and quicker trial-and-error, and retaining C++ for core architecture, performance and reusable systems. They also describe Blueprint graphs becoming difficult to test, modularize and merge. Suggested 60:40 or 70:30 C++/Blueprint mixes are contextual preferences, not experimentally optimized proportions. Perceived safer experimentation, immediate feedback and runtime performance claims remain participant testimony; this experiment measures neither crash rates nor frame time/energy.

The July package paper and current author PDF have the same headline outcome tables. The earlier abstract/conclusion argues for model/code coexistence; the later text emphasizes constrained visual structure and error prevention. The current discussion still says no positive/negative syntax comments arose while adding a constrained-structure explanation. The report supports perceived trial-and-error safety and hybrid practice, but provides no randomized separation of syntax, engine integration, familiarity, supplied scaffolding or task fit. **Neither edition identifies a pure mechanism effect.** The experiment has no randomized hybrid-workflow arm.

For the broader Nu survey, this is positive evidence that an integrated alternative can improve prepared game-function scores even among professional programmers, with concrete practitioner reasons to preserve access to both representations. It weakens a blanket inference that model-based tools merely add bureaucracy or are useful only to artists. It also sharpens the funder-relevant comparison: can an evolving interactive-software workflow preserve rapid feedback while controlling state, testability, merge and extension costs over consequential changes? That question must account for actual tool support and total work; this paper alone establishes no Nu advantage, first-study claim, F#/C# effect or D1 explicit-case benefit.
