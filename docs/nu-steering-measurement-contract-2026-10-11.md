# Future human steering: observations, outcomes and reliability

**Prepared 2026-10-11; R21 remains deferred.** This resolves the [agenda's](nu-research-agenda-2026-10-08.md#r21-human-steering-and-intervention--future-direction) request for a literature-grounded observation vocabulary. It is a candidate measurement contract, not a validated instrument, participant-number calculation or execution order. [PLAN](../PLAN.md) retains zero allocation and all construction, participant and worker holds. Mechanisms/costs and bounded agent maintenance remain the leading programme.

## Practical question and comparison

The eventual question is whether a specified Nu representation or observation facility helps people guide an agent to a correct, useful change with acceptable total work. The practical decision must name a beneficiary, task and credible alternative from the [provenance frame](nu-practice-and-task-provenance-2026-10-11.md). More prompting, fewer keystrokes or a preferred workflow is not automatically better steering.

Preserve two possible policies: **steering-only**, where humans specify, inspect, diagnose and redirect while agents implement; and **unrestricted intervention**, also permitting human editing and takeover. These answer different questions and need not become a factorial. Human-authored replacement code supplied through a prompt is human coding under either interface. If a future steering-only policy forbids that contribution, record violations and outcomes; do not relabel them as autonomous work or discard successful rescues silently.

Choose one consequential comparison before designing a study: for example, the same implementation policy and ordinary tools with a specified state-inspection facility available versus its credible reconstruction alternative. Treatment, training, permitted feedback and initial task information must be explicit. Human-only and agent-only conditions remain optional boundary references. Neither is a mandatory equal-weight arm or a substitute for the human-steering question.

## What the primary methods contribute

| Source | Useful evidence | Boundary for this contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [S229, PicoScenes](literature/full-reading/S229-picoscenes-expert-ai.md) | Favorable expert–AI development results and concrete specification, architecture, prompting, diagnosis and integration work | Four experienced developers in a specialized project; commit/feature timing and excluded agent-only failures do not identify a pure steering effect or complete adoption cost. |
| [S302, METR v2](literature/full-reading/S302-metr-developer-productivity.md) | Randomized AI permission with an adverse completion-time result; pre-assignment expectations and a detailed activity codebook | Permission differs from use; completed issues, usable recordings and developer beliefs have different denominators. The codebook reports no independent agreement statistic. |
| [S303, Grounded Copilot v3](literature/full-reading/S303-grounded-copilot.md) | Task-local planning/exploration modes, partial acceptance, repair, validation and mistaken reassurance from repeated suggestions | Interpretive coding and changing laboratory tasks do not establish productivity effects. Released time/event calculations need reconciliation before adopting numerical summaries. |

These sources support useful distinctions, with scoped favorable, adverse and qualitative evidence preserved. Their headline results must not be pooled. S302's separate later author update also qualifies temporal transfer; it supplies no validated 2027 effect. Familiarity, task selection, tools and contribution policies can change what a measure means.

## Observation vocabulary

Use a trace of **episodes, actions and outcomes**, linked by stable task, person, requirement, artifact and agent-attempt IDs. Record the evidence supporting a label: visible interaction, tool log, explicit participant statement, retrospective report or analyst interpretation. Keep unsupported intent unknown. The following are proposed fields, not observed Nu participant data.

| Proposed activity | Observable evidence to retain | Distinction that must survive coding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frame the requirement | Requested behavior, retained obligations, constraints, examples and scope revisions | Task selection versus later task expansion; a human-specified algorithm versus a high-level goal. |
| Gather and supply context | Source/docs/state inspected; context, examples or code supplied to the agent | Available, actually exposed and apparently used information; original code versus copied code versus human-authored replacement. |
| Delegate or redirect | Request, target artifact/subproblem, trigger, agent response and next action | Initial delegation, clarification, diagnosis, correction and strategy change. Prompt count alone does not measure their difficulty or value. |
| Inspect a proposal or state | Displayed suggestion/diff/runtime state; inspection interval; referenced requirement | Viewing, reported understanding and verified correctness. A background diagnostic is not proof that a person used it. |
| Compare alternatives | Alternatives considered, their provenance and relevant behavioral differences | Repeated generation versus an independently motivated requirement or solution. Agreement and ranking are observations about the interface, not correctness evidence. |
| Accept, reject or revise | Actual artifact change, partial selection, direct edit, subsequent reversion | Initial acceptance, later retention, manual repair and tested validity; preserve the sequence rather than collapsing it into one success label. |
| Verify behavior | Check invoked, visible result, covered obligation and subsequent decision | Static diagnostics, examples, traces and final assessment. Candidate-visible checks remain separate from hidden final scoring. |
| Rescue, hand off or stop | Takeover/change of actor, assistance, stopping reason and resulting artifact | Human rescue versus agent repair, voluntary completion versus timeout/abandonment, and unobserved outcome versus failure. |

Coding may need simultaneous tags: a person can review one result while another agent runs. If a primary-activity label is required for time accounting, state how mixed intervals are handled and retain overlap separately. Do not count an inactive display as no human thought, or force a planning/exploration interpretation from screen behavior alone. Participant reports can explain intent, but are not an independent measure of counterfactual speed or correctness.

## Outcome and cost boundary

| Quantity | Proposed unit and boundary | Interpretation limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Correct change and retained behavior | Independently checked obligations per **assigned task**, with versions, horizon and missingness from R05/R09 | Accepted output, compilation, line count and self-reported confidence are different endpoints. Correct alternatives remain valid. |
| Human work | Active person-time in specification, preparation, inspection, diagnosis, editing, verification, rescue and rework; assistance identified by actor | Sum person-time across people, but do not count the same person's overlapping activities twice. Prior setup/training and later upkeep need their own scope. |
| Elapsed time | Assignment/eligible-start through declared delivery or stopping, with waits and interruptions identified | Lead time is not active work; deadlines and outside interruptions cannot silently become productivity effects. |
| Agent and environment cost | Model/tool work, waiting, retries, setup and observation/evaluation cost under R06/R08 | Parallel agent durations cannot simply be added to human elapsed time; total resource spend and critical-path delay differ. |
| Process and experience | Redirection/rescue episodes, stated confidence, perceived effort and reasons for use/non-use | These may explain mechanisms or acceptability; no unvalidated composite steering score or automatic benefit conclusion. |

Preserve a flow from eligible/invited people and available tasks to submitted, assigned, attempted, delivered and assessable work. Record known refusal, non-use, noncompliance, recording loss and noncompletion reasons. Unobserved reasons stay unknown. A comparison among successful recorded cases answers a narrower question than outcomes for all assigned work; report the restriction rather than presenting it as a treatment effect for everyone.

A later protocol can choose verified attainment within a common budget, or time to a declared correct-completion criterion with unfinished work handled explicitly. State the primary decision and analysis before observations. Do not choose whichever endpoint favors Nu, divide costs only by successful runs without qualification, or adjust away post-assignment rescue/activity and call the remainder a pure representation effect. Repeated tasks, episodes and prompts are nested within people; more prompts do not create more independent participants.

## Concrete boundaries from the prepared Nu cases

For the [R10 reward-edit case](nu-live-edit-contract-2026-10-11.md), asking for a new reward, supplying the field-migration mapping, diagnosing a reset and manually restoring progress are different contributions. A reload event does not establish preservation. Record the intended mapping, observed progress and checked future reward separately; someone who supplies the implementation through a prompt has contributed code regardless of who writes the file.

For [R03 history recovery](nu-history-application-contract-2026-10-11.md), choosing a checkpoint, inspecting represented state and checking continued behavior are different actions. Successful B0 restoration does not establish B1 continuation or the person's understanding of shared references. These are examples of applying an observation contract to existing authored cases, not verified steering tasks or participant observations.

## Reliability and promotion conditions

Before any effect study, a separately authorized instrument/feasibility stage would need to resolve the following:

1. **Coverage and operational definitions.** Test whether the proposed fields distinguish plausible steering, editing, automated activity and unknown cases across the selected tasks and tools. Preserve new categories and counterexamples rather than forcing a binary mode.
2. **Independent annotation.** Establish a codebook and boundaries, then have independent annotators label held-out material before adjudication. Report disagreements, unclassifiable intervals, timing differences and an appropriate agreement assessment, including categories that fail. Shared discussion or repeated AI outputs alone cannot establish independence.
3. **Outcome linkage.** Check whether an action label corresponds to a documented contribution or obligation while keeping final scoring separate. A reliable count of prompts can still be an invalid measure of effectiveness. Test timestamp units, overlapping intervals and aggregation on known records before using any timing summary.
4. **Sampling and observation effects.** Establish whose work and which requirements are omitted by recruitment, task submission, recording and stopping. Think-aloud, recording, task guidance and paid support can change behavior. A fixed task reduces some selection while narrowing realism; neither design is automatically superior.
5. **Practical estimand and precision.** Choose the beneficiary's meaningful difference, task/person variation, assignment unit and tolerable uncertainty before a participant-number calculation. A small descriptive study may inform the instrument later; it cannot establish a population effect or justify the same number for a different design.
